You paid for the website build. Then the renewal invoices start showing up, and nobody can tell you whether $50 a month or $500 a month is a normal price to keep the site running.

The big guides don't settle it. One quotes anything from $5 to $25,000 or more a month, and few show where their numbers come from. So we priced website maintenance cost line by line on four platforms, using official pricing pages checked on October 30, 2026, and lined up what five top-ranking guides say.

## The short answer: website maintenance cost in 2026

For a typical startup or small business site, the software side of website maintenance costs about $120 to $350 a year. Published guides put the full bill at $30 to $500 a month, and above the floor, almost all of it is people time. A Webflow site with two hours of help a month runs about $2,710 a year.

## Why website maintenance cost estimates are all over the place

Several of the guides we checked blend two very different costs into one number: the software bills and the people who do the work. Software is cheap and predictable. People time is where the range blows up.

We lined up the small business figures from five widely read guides, all showing up in Google results for this search this week.

![Range chart comparing monthly website maintenance cost for a small business site across five published guides in 2026](/journal/website-maintenance-cost-guides-compared.webp "website-maintenance-cost-guides-compared")

The guides roughly agree on the floor, around $30 to $50 a month. The ceiling swings from $100 to $500, depending on which one you read.

Look closer and the agreement is thinner than it seems. UENI's chart reuses GoDaddy's ranges, so it isn't a second opinion. One guide lists domain renewal at $10 to $130 a year in a table and $0.99 to $60 a year in its text.

None of the five prices Webflow, Framer, or Next.js at today's rates either. WP Buffs quotes Webflow plans from its 2024 update, and Webflow rebuilt its plans in May 2026.

## What the software costs, by platform

This is the part you can predict to the dollar. We priced a typical startup marketing site: about 20 pages, a small blog, one language, and one custom domain. These are list prices in USD, billed yearly.

![Bar chart of yearly website maintenance software cost on Framer, Next.js, Webflow, and WordPress in 2026, from $120 to $346](/journal/website-maintenance-cost-by-platform-2026.webp "website-maintenance-cost-by-platform-2026")

Plan prices come from [Webflow's pricing page](https://webflow.com/pricing), Framer, WP Engine, and [Vercel's pricing page](https://vercel.com/pricing). The domain figure is the at-cost .com price through Cloudflare Registrar, about $10.46 a year. UptimeRobot's free plan covers 50 monitors with 5-minute checks and allows commercial use.

### Framer and Webflow: the platform carries the upkeep

On a hosted builder, hosting and server updates come with the subscription. Webflow needs the $25 Premium plan once you want a blog, since Basic has no CMS. Framer Basic includes 1,000 CMS items and 30 pages, which covers a small site.

Your maintenance here is mostly content: editing pages, fixing broken links, and checking that forms still reach your inbox.

### WordPress: cheap to host, more work to keep current

WordPress shipped version 7.1.4 on October 28, 2026, and the [WordPress releases page](https://wordpress.org/download/releases/) says only the most recent version in the 7.1 series is safe to use. Every core, plugin, and theme update needs someone to apply it and check that nothing broke.

That recurring work is why WordPress usually costs more in people time than its hosting bill suggests. Premium plugin and theme licenses add to the software line too, and they vary by site.

### Next.js: low bills, but every change needs a developer

Vercel's Hobby plan is for personal, non-commercial use, so a company site belongs on Pro at $20 per developer seat a month, which includes $20 of usage credit. The hosting is cheap.

The catch is that copy changes and dependency updates need a developer unless you connect a CMS your team can edit on its own.

## The real website maintenance cost is people time

Once the software is paid for, the rest of the bill is hours. GoDaddy puts developer rates at $50 to $150 or more an hour, and WebFX lists $150 an hour for its own team. GoDaddy puts maintenance retainers at $100 to $1,500 or more a month.

Here's a simple formula that works for any platform: yearly software cost, plus hours a month times your hourly rate times 12.

| Platform | Hours a month we'd budget | Per month at $50 an hour | Per month at $150 an hour |

|---|---|---|---|

| Framer or Webflow | 1 to 2 | $50 to $100 | $150 to $300 |

| WordPress | 2 to 4 | $100 to $200 | $300 to $600 |

| Next.js without a CMS | 2 to 4 | $100 to $200 | $300 to $600 |

Run the numbers on a Webflow site with two hours of help a month at $100 an hour. That's $2,400 a year in people time and about $310 in software, so roughly $2,710 a year in total. The software is about a tenth of the bill.

### What those hours should cover

- Content edits: blog posts, team changes, pricing updates, new case studies

- Updates: core, plugin, and dependency updates on WordPress and Next.js

- Checks: forms, broken links, mobile speed, and Search Console errors

- Backups: making sure a recent backup exists and actually restores

- Small improvements: a new landing page section or a sharper call to action

## Our take: how to keep maintenance costs low

### Pick a platform your team can edit

The cheapest hour of maintenance is the one you don't have to buy. If your marketing team can update pages without a developer, most of the monthly work disappears. Our [Webflow vs Next.js comparison](https://www.natwic.com/journal/webflow-vs-next.js-for-startups-which-to-pick-in-2026) covers which platform fits which team.

### Keep the plugin list short

Every plugin, integration, and script is something that can break on update day. On WordPress especially, fewer plugins means fewer updates to test and fewer late-night surprises.

### Buy named tasks, not a vague plan

Before you sign a care plan, ask what's included in plain terms: how many hours, which tasks, and how fast they respond. "Unlimited small changes" usually has a limit somewhere in the fine print.

### Recheck your renewals every year

Platform prices move. Webflow rebuilt its plans in May 2026, which changed what many sites pay. A 15-minute review each year keeps you from paying for a plan you outgrew or no longer need.

## FAQ

### How much does website maintenance cost per month?

For a small business site, published guides range from $30 to $500 a month. The software alone usually runs $10 to $30 a month on Framer, Webflow, WordPress, or Next.js, more on WordPress if you pay for premium plugins. The rest depends on how many hours of help you need and what that help costs.

### What does website maintenance include?

It covers hosting and domain renewals, software updates, security, backups, uptime monitoring, and content edits. On hosted platforms like Webflow and Framer, the platform handles server updates, so your maintenance is mostly content and checks.

### Is Webflow cheaper to maintain than WordPress?

In software, they're close: about $310 a year for Webflow Premium and about $346 a year for WordPress on WP Engine's entry plan. In people time, Webflow usually costs less, because there are no plugin or core updates to apply and test each month.

### Can I maintain my website myself?

Yes, if you're on a platform your team can edit and you work through a checklist like the one above. Plan for outside help with WordPress updates, Next.js code changes, and anything that touches forms or integrations.

### How much should I budget for maintenance compared with the build?

WP Buffs cites an estimate that a yearly maintenance contract costs 15 to 20 percent of the original development cost. We'd use that as a ceiling check, not a target, since a well-built site on an editable platform often needs less.

## Budget your website maintenance cost before the renewals hit

Website maintenance cost comes down to two numbers: a small, predictable software bill and the hours of help you buy. Pick a platform your team can edit, keep the moving parts few, and review your renewals once a year.
